# WS5: Go-to-Market

**Summary**
1. **Anchor on the transaction.** Inspectors produce the failing number, agents decide who gets called, and transaction coordinators (TCs) chase the paperwork. Win by making each of them look good inside a tight inspection window.
2. **Don't do the initial transaction test.** TN home inspectors may not accept commissions from parties dealing with their client (Rule 0780-05-12), and a mitigator that tests invites a conflict-of-interest question. Staying mitigation-only makes it safe for inspectors to refer, and they get **no referral fees**.
3. **Offer:** a written, priced quote within 2 business hours from the test report and photos; install within 5 business days; post-mitigation result guaranteed below 4.0 pCi/L with an independent-test option; lifetime workmanship warranty; a written design sheet with every job.
4. **Recurring revenue:** 2-year retest reminders and fan-age tracking, built into the ops stack from job #1.
5. **Cross-sell:** crawlspace vapor barrier and encapsulation. In Knox, jobs of $3,000+ need a Home Improvement license first (WS1).

> Names below come from public web listings seen in search results. **No one has been contacted.** Templates are drafts for the owner to review. No reviews, testimonials, or endorsements are included, and none should be invented.

## 1. Channel map

| Channel | Role in the deal | What they want | Our hook | Rules and constraints |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Home inspectors** (run most transaction radon tests) | Deliver the ≥4.0 result to the buyer | Keep agents happy; avoid liability; don't look like they steer work | A one-page "what happens next" sheet they can attach to failing reports; a mitigator that never competes for their test | TN Rule 0780-05-12: no commissions or allowances from parties dealing with the client ([rule PDF](https://publications.tnsosfiles.com/rules/0780/0780-05/0780-05-12.20240323.pdf)). Inspectors may not do repair work on homes they inspected within 12 months (T.C.A. § 62-6-308, [Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-62/chapter-6/part-3/section-62-6-308/)), which is why they need someone to hand off to. **No referral fees** |
| **Buyer's agents** | Write the repair request | A fast, credible price to put in the repair proposal; no deal delays | Quote from the report plus photos in about 2 h; a written scope they can attach | Referral fees to or from licensees: TN license law bars licensees paying referral fees to non-licensees, and RESPA may apply. **Pay no one for referrals** until an attorney confirms |
| **Listing agents** | Push the seller to fix rather than credit | Close on time; a seller who isn't angry | Install inside the resolution window; a certificate-style report for the next buyer | Same as above |
| **Transaction coordinators** | Chase every repair receipt | One email with invoice, design sheet, and post-test | An "agent packet" auto-generated at job close (WS6) | None specific |
| **Direct to homeowner** | Existing owners, winter testing season | Trust; clear price | Published price bands; January (Radon Action Month) campaign; activation offer for Knox homes with passive stacks | Keep marketing claims factual. Health-risk statements should cite EPA |

**Test-and-mitigate conflict (context):** the NRPP Code of Ethics requires professionals to "avoid conflicts of interest… and openly disclose such conflicts" (NRPP candidate handbook, [PDF](https://nrpp.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Candidate-Handbook_UPDATED_05-04-2023.pdf)). State programs such as North Carolina note that the conflict "can be avoided by using an independent tester" ([NC DHHS](https://www.ncdhhs.gov/divisions/health-service-regulation/north-carolina-radon-program/radon-mitigation)). For post-mitigation tests in a transaction, offer the choice of **your own CRM result plus an independent test by the buyer's inspector**.

## 2. Offer design

| Element | Spec | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Quote speed | Written, priced quote ≤2 business hours after receiving the test report plus 6 photos (foundation, sump, slab cracks, attic access, exterior wall, electrical panel) | Contingency windows are negotiated per contract. The TN Realtors RF401 leaves the inspection-period days blank for the parties to fill in ([RF401](https://clarksvilleaor.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/RF401-6.pdf)), so speed matters in every deal |
| Price transparency | Published bands on the website: slab/basement from $X; crawlspace from $Y; combination from $Z. Final quote after the site visit or PFE | No local competitor publishes prices except Radon 1 (WS3). Bands come from WS4 (about $1,600, $2,200, and $2,500 floors) |
| Install speed | ≤5 business days from signed quote | Fits typical resolution windows |
| Result guarantee | Post-mitigation result <4.0 pCi/L, or additional work free (match Radon't's <2.7 claim once there's data to back it) | Market floor (WS3) |
| Warranty | Lifetime workmanship (transferable to the next owner); fan per manufacturer (5–7 yr, [Rn86 FAQ](https://rn86solutions.com/radon-testing-and-mitigation-faqs/)) | Market floor |
| Documentation | Design sheet (suction points, PFE readings, fan model and operating point on the fan curve), before/after readings, manometer photo, labeled disconnect photo | The engineering differentiator, made visible |
| Post-test | Included (short-term kit or CRM) no sooner than 24 h after install, per common protocol (**verify the exact timing in ANSI/AARST SGM-SF**) | EPA recommends a post-mitigation test within 30 days (brief) |

## 3. Recurring program
- **Retest reminder at 24 months** (EPA guidance, from the brief), sent as a pre-paid AirChek kit offer (about $18 cost, [Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/ip/AirChek-Short-Term-Charcoal-Radon-Test-Kit/843078229)). Suggested price $49–79 (estimated).
- **Fan-age tracking:** flag every fan at year 5 (warranty end) and year 10 (typical life). Offer a fan replacement at a published price. Radon 1 lists $300–900 or $650–900 ⚠ (WS3).
- **New-owner handoff:** when a mitigated house resells, the listing agent gets the original design sheet and warranty transfer. This is the next buyer's first touchpoint.

## 4. Cross-sell: crawlspace vapor barrier and encapsulation
- East TN has many crawlspace foundations ([knoxvillehomeinspections.org](https://knoxvillehomeinspections.org/blog/radon-east-tennessee/)). A sub-membrane radon system is already half an encapsulation.
- Upsell path: 12-mil → 20-mil liner, wall liner, and a dehumidifier. Material deltas: 12-mil at $308 vs 20-mil at $560 per 1,200 sf ([Crawlspace Depot](https://crawlspacedepot.com/vapor-barriers/)).
- **Compliance gate:** in Knox, a job at $3,000 or more requires the TN Home Improvement license ([TN BLC](https://www.tn.gov/commerce/regboards/contractors/license/get/home-improvement.html)). Don't quote encapsulation packages at $3,000+ in Knox until it's issued.

| 6–10 | Owner-reviewed outreach to 10 inspectors and 10 agents from §6 using the §7 templates. Ask each inspector for their radon attach and fail rates (feeds WS2) | First referral relationships |
| 8–12 | Offer a lunch-and-learn CE-style talk to one brokerage office ("reading a radon report in 10 minutes"). Start retargeting and Google search ads at a small budget (~$300/mo, WS4 fixed) | Pipeline |
| 12 | Review against the gate: jobs/month, average ticket, CM, quote-to-close rate, time-to-quote | Go / adjust |

## 7. Message templates (drafts for owner review; do not send as-is)

**A. Inspector intro email**
> Subject: A mitigation hand-off that won't compete with your radon tests
>
> Hi [Name], I'm [Owner], an NRPP-certified radon mitigation specialist based in [South Knoxville/Maryville]. I only do mitigation. I don't sell radon tests, so I'll never be competing with you for the test.
>
> When one of your reports comes back at 4.0 or higher, your client (or their agent) can send me the report and a few photos and get a written, priced quote within two business hours. Every system gets a written design sheet and a post-mitigation result.
>
> I don't pay or accept referral fees. I just want to be an easy name to pass along. I've attached a one-page "Your radon result is high. What now?" sheet you're welcome to include with reports. Would a 15-minute call be useful?
>
> [Owner] · [NRPP #] · [phone] · [site]

**B. Agent one-liner (for text or email after a failing test)**
> Radon over 4.0 on [address]? Send me the report and 6 photos. You'll have a written, priced scope for your repair proposal in about 2 hours, and install within 5 business days. [link to upload form]

**C. Leave-behind sheet outline:** "Your radon result is 4.0 or higher: what happens next." (1) What the number means (cite EPA). (2) Typical fixes by foundation type. (3) Our price bands. (4) Timeline. (5) What you'll receive (design sheet, post-test, warranty). (6) How to send photos. No testimonials until real ones exist.

**D. 2-year retest reminder**
> It's been two years since we installed the radon system at [address]. EPA recommends retesting every two years. Reply YES and we'll mail a lab-analyzed test kit for $[X], or book a monitor visit. Your fan is [age] years old, and its warranty [ends/ended] on [date].
