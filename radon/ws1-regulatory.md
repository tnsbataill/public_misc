# WS1: Regulatory and Licensing, Knox and Blount Counties, TN

**Summary**
1. **No state radon license was found.** EPA's list of states with their own radon credentialing programs does not include TN. TDEC only *recommends* NRPP or NRSB certification. The NRPP-listing text saying "TN requires state licensing" appears to be wrong, but this is still unresolved until TDEC confirms it (see §2).
2. **NRPP Radon Mitigation Specialist is the credential to get.** It costs about $800–975 all-in (24-hour course, $175 exam, $275–300 certification fee) and takes about 2–6 weeks, mostly waiting on the exam and the application.
3. **Home Improvement license: depends.** Knox is one of 9 counties that adopted it, and it applies to residential jobs of $3,000–24,999. Most radon jobs come in under $3,000, but crawlspace encapsulation cross-sells and large combination jobs would cross the threshold. Blount has not adopted it.
4. **Electrical is the real compliance issue.** A hardwired fan with a disconnect is electrical work. Knox (a local codes jurisdiction) reportedly does not accept the state LLE license. Blount appears to be state-inspected, where an LLE (about $155) works. Plan either to plug the fan into an existing outlet or to subcontract a licensed electrician in Knox.
5. **Compliance fits the gate.** First-year compliance costs about $2.5–5K and takes 1–2 months, which is inside the "≤3 months / ≤$5K" test. Insurance is the largest item.

> **How sources were checked.** This environment's network policy blocked direct page loads (tn.gov, epa.gov, nrpp.info, and every other host tried), so every fact below was read from **web-search result excerpts** of the cited page, not from the page itself. The URLs are the primary pages, but each row should be spot-checked in a browser before it is relied on. Rows marked ⚠ have conflicting sources.

## 2. Conflict: does TN license radon contractors? ⚠

| Says "licensed by the state" | Says "no state license" |
|---|---|
| NRPP professional listings, e.g. [nrpp.info/listings/bl-13589](https://nrpp.info/listings/bl-13589.html), carry boilerplate reading "the state of Tennessee requires that all contractors performing radon related services within the state, be licensed by the state" | [EPA supplemental info](https://www.epa.gov/radon/epa-map-radon-zones-and-supplemental-information): states with credentialing programs are CA, CO, CT, FL, IL, IN, IA, KS, KY, ME, MN, NE, NH, NJ, OH, PA, RI, UT, VA, WV. **TN is not on the list.** |
| [getlicensemap.com](https://getlicensemap.com/states/radon/tennessee) (aggregator): "TN requires a state license… $500,000 liability… 8 h CE/yr" | [TDEC Radon Program](https://www.tn.gov/environment/stewardship/programs/radon.html): refers residents to the NRPP and NRSB directories and "highly recommends" a certified professional. No license application was found |
| | [CERTI TN fact sheet](https://certi.us/wp-content/images/Tennessee_Radon_Fact_Sheet_08.31.22.pdf) and other training providers: no state requirement |

**Reading:** The "licensed by the state" wording is most likely a general *contractor* licensing reminder (rows 3–5 above), not a radon-specific license. The aggregator's "$500K / 8 h CE" figures don't match any TN page found.
**To resolve (owner action, since agents may not contact anyone):** email the TN Radon Program (the EPA state contact listed is TDEC; the program hotline is 1-800-232-1139) and ask one question: *"Is any state license or registration required to perform radon mitigation for compensation in Tennessee?"* Keep the written reply on file.

## 3. Electrical: options and open questions ⚠

What the standards and code require (sourced from [AARST RRNC-2020](https://standards.aarst.org/RRNC-2020/17/) and [CC-1000](https://standards.aarst.org/CC-1000-2018/19/) excerpts):
- A branch-circuit outlet must be within 6 ft of the fan, and the breaker must be labeled.
- A fan that isn't cord-and-plug connected needs a disconnect within sight of the fan, labeled "Radon Fan."
- Plug-connected fans may not be used outdoors.

Who may do the wiring:
- A TN homeowner may pull a permit for their own home. A contractor needs a Title 62 Ch. 6 license ([TN Electrical Permit FAQ](https://www.tn.gov/commerce/fire/permit/electrical/faq.html)).
- **Knox:** permits inside Knoxville city limits come from the City of Knoxville. Per a secondary source, [tennesseeelectricalauthority.com](https://tennesseeelectricalauthority.com/tennessee-electrical-licensing-requirements), the LLE is *not accepted* in Knox and similar local-code jurisdictions. **Unverified against a Knox primary page.**
- **Blount:** the state Deputy Electrical Inspector list covers Blount, and Alcoa moved to state permits in 2020 ([Alcoa electrical permits](https://www.cityofalcoa-tn.gov/182/Electrical-Permits)), so an LLE is probably valid there.

Practical paths:
1. **Attic or interior fan on an existing receptacle.** Plug-connected with no new wiring, so no electrical permit is likely needed. Only works when a receptacle is already within 6 ft.
2. **Get an LLE.** About $155 and a 40-question exam. Covers Blount-type jurisdictions only.
3. **Subcontract a licensed electrician for exterior or hardwired fans in Knox.** Modeled in WS4 as $150 blended per job (estimated).

Questions for the owner to confirm: Knox County Codes and City of Knoxville (a) whether an electrical license is required for a radon-fan disconnect and whip, and (b) whether a building permit is required for a radon system.

## 4. Home Improvement license: what triggers it

- **Threshold:** total residential project value of $3,000–24,999, in adopting counties only: Bradley, Davidson, Hamilton, Haywood, **Knox**, Marion, Robertson, Rutherford, Shelby ([TN BLC](https://www.tn.gov/commerce/regboards/contractors/license/get/home-improvement.html)).
- **Radon at $1,200–2,500 in Knox:** not triggered.
- **Combination jobs at $3,000+ or crawlspace encapsulation in Knox:** triggered. Get the license before offering encapsulation (WS5 cross-sell).
- **Blount:** not triggered at any radon price point.
- The license also requires proof of workers' comp. TN's construction-industry workers' comp rules differ from other industries, and an owner with no employees may qualify for an exemption. **Not researched in this pass. Verify with TN Labor & Workforce Development before applying.**
